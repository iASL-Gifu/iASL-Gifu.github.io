## 自己紹介
I am a PhD researcher at Gifu University, Japan, specializing in real-time event-based perception using Spiking Neural Networks for autonomous driving under adverse weather conditions.

My research focuses on developing energy-efficient and robust perception systems capable of detecting vehicles, pedestrians, and environmental hazards in challenging conditions such as rain, fog, and snow. I work at the intersection of neuromorphic computing, event-based vision, adaptive sensor fusion, machine learning, and intelligent autonomous systems.

I hold academic backgrounds in Mechatronic Engineering and Railway Engineering and have more than five years of experience in sensor-based monitoring, transportation systems, predictive maintenance, machine learning, engineering education, and infrastructure project management.

My previous research involved developing real-time systems for measuring railway wheel-flange wear using inductive displacement and temperature sensors. The work combined sensor design, signal processing, vehicle motion, vibrations, track irregularities, environmental compensation, and machine-learning-based prediction to support improved railway safety and maintenance decision-making.

I have contributed to peer-reviewed research published in Transportation Research Record and through the American Society of Mechanical Engineers. My professional experience includes working as a university lecturer, researcher, teaching assistant, mechatronics engineer, project coordinator, and construction supervisor.
